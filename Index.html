Our Mission

MEAT is building the future of how people connect, grow, and experience hospitality.

We create experiences, develop operators, tell stories, and build technology that brings people together.

We never set out to be Meateocre. MEAT Hospitality Group was born from watching too many great operators and chefs risk their dreams, hoping people would show up. We set out to change that.

We exist to unify meaningful experiences under one company with one shared mission.

Every idea we build, every experience we create, and every step we take is guided by the same purpose: to bring people together, inspire them to take their first step, and grow alongside them.

We don't build disconnected concepts. We build one ecosystem where every part belongs to the same story.

Because when people grow, we grow.

We want the MEAT Universe to be something people of every age carry with them into their everyday decisions — and a reminder that we're all in this together when life feels heavy. The same way the shows and movies we grew up with stayed with us, we want everything MEAT puts out, now and in the years ahead, to be a guide people carry for a lifetime.

The Meat App

Making experiences universally accessible.

"Your wallet shouldn't be the only thing determining which rooms you get access to."

In short: do the everyday things you already do. Earn Meat Points. Redeem them across dinners, cafes, gyms, and more.

Too many great experiences are gated behind a paywall — reserved for whoever can already afford them. We don't think access should work that way. It shouldn't matter who you know or what you can spend. It should matter that you're willing to take the first step.

That's why we're building Meat Points — earned not just by spending, but by living. Getting up and out of the house. Talking to someone new. Starting the thing you've been putting off. Asking the question you've been too nervous to ask. Every step forward earns you points, and points unlock access — a seat at a dinner, entry into new tiers, experiences that used to be reserved for whoever could already afford them. The reward for growing is more access to grow.

Every verified check-in earns 10 Meat Points — and if you complete it with a meatie, that jumps to 20. Show up for your life, and the points add up fast.

Just Keep Meating reminders send a quick nudge in the morning to get you excited for your tasks that day — "Good morning. Just Keep Meating — here are your reminders for today."

Those points live inside MEAT Rewards — and it's not just for MEAT events and dinners. Restaurants, cafes, bakeries, coffee shops, chef spotlights, unannounced concepts still in the works — even ground-floor preorder access to what's opening next. Why should you only get rewarded for doing one thing?

Preorder one of those ground-floor experiences and you'll see who else is waiting alongside you — building the hype before the place even opens its doors.

Gyms. Spas. Saunas. Day trips. The everyday things nobody else is rewarding you for. The bigger we grow, the more we'll add. Welcome to everyday living as it should be.

Here's how it works: do it on your own, or connect with a meatie who's doing something similar — complete the task, verify it happened, then move on to your next objective. No hollow self-checkboxes. Real steps, confirmed by real people.

We want to reward you for what you do every day, with things you already do. Went grocery shopping? That's life. Met a friend for coffee? Life. Went on a date, walked the dog, tried a new restaurant, worked on your idea, finally sent the email you've been avoiding? All life.

You don't necessarily have to do it alone. See what others are up to. Connect based on shared interest. Hold each other accountable. Complete the task together — or do it on your own. Verify each other's progress. Earn Meat Points. Keep moving. Either way, you earn points for taking that first step. We encourage you to go with how you feel and what you like — every activity is based on your interests. Someone should always be able to take the first step alone and still have it count — the connection is there for when you don't want to go it solo, not a requirement to earn access.

When you sign up, a short survey gets to know your interests — then we show you meaties who match your interests, so you can connect and share what you're up to or what you'd like to accomplish. Share, message, laugh, and connect. That's the Meat way.

Our goal is to create a Meatsperience you'll be talking about for years to come. Once you sign up, you're part of the Meat family — for life.

We're not rewarding spending. We're rewarding participation in life. Go somewhere. Do something. Meet someone. Take the first step. MEAT rewards you for participating — and Meat Points turn participation into possibility.

"We're not trying to get people to spend more time on an app. We're trying to use the app to get people to spend more time living."

Welcome to a new era of rewards — and a new way to live your everyday life.

Because waking up is hard enough. Let's reward you for what you do every day.

The Blueprint · A Book by Holden Krivelin

Nine years. Walking, calling, talking, going into places I once stood outside of — too afraid to walk in. I held back. I didn't want anyone to see me. And the thought of being around other people, in those rooms, felt like I didn't belong — like my presence alone might offend someone.

But I kept going.

And I've seen it through every set of eyes.

The host — smile on, battles hidden. The bartender — serious on the surface, something softer underneath. The chef — always running. Toward something or away from it, hard to tell. And the owner — for some, this is the dream they've been chasing their whole life. Between the concept and the first night of service, there are surprising setbacks nobody warned them about. Things you only discover when you're already in it — and by then, you just have to keep going. And then there are those for whom this isn't the first time. The challenge shifts — can we top what we built before? For some, yes. For others, the second chapter never quite finds the same room.

This book is for all of them.

From the Pages

My dad's friends were my friends.

Every week, same routine. Dunkin' Donuts first. Irish Dave would sit there with his lottery tickets — he was in his eighties and still gambling like it was his job. If he won, he'd take us to the Italian restaurant we liked. I always got the eggplant parmesan.

There was the Broadway Diner once — I got a salmon burger that was so good we talked about it for years, even though we never went back. And Burger King, where Billy snuck me a cheeseburger because my dad wouldn't allow it. I ate it in the car like it was the greatest thing I'd ever tasted.

A woman looked at me once at Burger King and said — why are you hanging out with those old people? Why don't you come hang out with me and my daughter?

I didn't have an answer for her. Those were just my people.

Me, my dad, Irish Dave, Billy, Jimmy. Same routine every week. Same seats. Same orders. Same laughs.

Until Dave and Billy had a falling out and never recovered. That's how some friendships go. You share a hundred Dunkin' Donuts coffees and then one thing changes everything.

But while it lasted — it was everything.

There was a cat named Snowball. She was my world from the start — I picked her up and gave her more kisses than any animal I've ever known. She came with us to Florida. She was family.

When my dad got sick, everything moved fast. Bile duct cancer. Ten days home. And then he was gone.

Snowball went to live with Carol, my dad's ex. I had no choice. You can always tell when an animal loses someone — they're never quite the same after.

I found out my dad passed while walking down a hill to pay his bills. Just another day, I thought. And then the call came.

One moment. And everything shifts.

That's what this book is really about.

The MEAT Method
Not a sales book. A life book.

Dedicated to my dad.
A.K.A. Pops. A.K.A. Chewbacca.

He showed me through our drives to Starbucks. Through the pickups at every odd job I held until they tried to get rid of me. Through the late nights coming home from security — walking in to find him laying down, small TV on, and kissing him on the cheek with a hug attached.

He tried to teach me through books. But I wasn't built that way. I was a visual learner and I knew it. So I watched instead. Every move was studied. Every word was absorbed. Every report was watched.

He was the teacher. I was always the student.

There's one moment I'll never forget.

We were driving to a friend's house and got lost on the highway. My brother was in the back seat. And as we drove straight ahead, the highway opened up into the biggest, brightest yellow sunset I'd ever seen. Like something out of a movie. Like end credits rolling as we drove right into it.

We ended up lost in the wrong part of town — and I had to tell him to keep moving. Don't stop here, Dad. And he didn't. He just kept driving. Calm. Unbothered. Like he always was.

And Air Supply was playing the entire ride.

He never had a favorite band. Never favored anything. He liked the way they sounded — and that was enough. He played them in the car in Florida. On drives with my grandmother. Every time we were together and moving somewhere.

That was his anthem. The band that could play him out at any occasion. Any monumental moment. That was it.

To this day, I still listen to Air Supply like clockwork. Two songs. The same two. And every single time, I think of him. Only because of him.

The sunset. The highway. The music. My dad.

That's the scene I carry with me everywhere I go.

He never had a favorite. Never favored anything or anyone. He was always into himself — impressed by himself. Nobody else. And everywhere he went, people were mesmerized. A gift of God, literally.

I said to him once — the lady in the hair salon, why don't you ask her out, Dad? He wasn't interested. He just knew he could captivate people. And that was the most beautiful thing about him.

You know you have a gift when you don't need to impress anybody — but you do it because you love it.

And you know you can make people laugh when you don't laugh at the joke. He was that guy. The funniest person I've ever known — and he would never laugh at his own jokes. Kept a straight face entirely. That was his move. That was his signature. That was the truest thing about him.

He once said to me — if we become famous, he doesn't want to be known. He wants me to take all the credit and run the nonprofit.

That's who he was. Never needed the spotlight. Just needed to know I had it.

And now look — I'm building it, Pops. Just like you said.

This one's for you.

Chapter 1 — Take the Small Piece

You don't need the whole opportunity. You only need a small piece — just enough to get in the door. What you do with that opportunity determines everything that comes next.

Life is made up in small bites.

You don't need the whole room. You don't need the perfect pitch. You don't need to steal the show. You just need one line. One piece of paper. One small moment that makes someone lean in and say — wait, who is this person?

Plant the seed. Get them curious. Give them just enough to take a look. And then do great work with whatever piece they hand you — because that small piece is the door. And once you're in the door, everything changes.

I learned this not in a boardroom. Not in a business school. I learned it in restaurants. Walking in when I was afraid to. Talking to owners, chefs, bartenders, hosts — people who built something from nothing and kept it alive one conversation at a time.

But if I'm being honest — I learned it first from watching my dad. He never had a course. Never had a formula. He just showed up, did the best he could, and left something behind that was bigger than anything material. He taught me to study people. Study what they do. Study their wrongs and make yours right. You don't have to be the smartest in the room — you just have to open your ears, sit there, and let life show you.

Last is first. First is last.
Every conversation plants a seed.
Every follow-up is the sale.
Every small bite compounds into something bigger than you imagined.

This isn't about hospitality. This is about life.

Life is meant to happen with you — not at you.

Matching never means automatic access to someone. MEAT can recommend meaties based on overlapping interests and goals, but each person chooses whether to connect, message, or accept an accountability request — comfortable and consensual, every time.

One's journey from childhood to meathood — a look inside how one's growth is different from everyone else's

◆

We build because we want to see the change we want others to experience.

For far too long, things have gone the same way. People have been turned away for being too different. Companies have relied on old tactics and called it innovation. The ones who didn't fit the mold were told to shrink themselves, to be quieter, to wait their turn.

I was one of those people.

This book is the story of what happens when you don't wait. When you can't wait. When the rejection becomes the reason, and the reason becomes the mission.

MEAT Hospitality Group was built on rejection — and it's dedicated to every person who has a dream and doesn't know where to begin.

Because I've been there. I know what it feels like to stand outside the room, too afraid to walk in. To feel like your presence alone might offend someone. To grow up faster than everyone around you, to be different in ways you couldn't explain, and to carry that difference like a weight instead of a gift.

This isn't just my story.
It's yours too.

Becoming something not learned — but explored, experienced, and still learning as I go.

Page One

I never judged my success by my grades.

Not because I didn't care — but because I was never thinking inside the classroom. I was thinking beyond it. No direction. Just a feeling. A connection to the outside world. To places. To people. And to the stories that came with every adventure.

I graduated high school. Barely.

And I wasn't ashamed of it. Because while everyone else was thinking about what was on the test, I was thinking about what was outside the window. The wind blowing. Mansions as big as the sky. A world that seemed bigger than anything a textbook could contain.

Florida was different. Delray Beach was different. Slower than the movies. Quieter than anything I'd known. Five dollars — a small cup of popcorn and a small lemonade — that was the thing we most looked forward to. That and the clubhouse.

I was the youngest in the community. My grandma's apartment. My grandpa had died in 2004 from heart-related issues, and we moved down to keep her company. Thought it would be cheaper to live. The mortgage was cheap — but the maintenance was a lot. It kept my dad in a panic most days.

I was eighteen. Freshly out of high school. No plan. No direction. Just the wind. The mansions. The popcorn. The lemonade. The clubhouse.

And a feeling that the world had a lot to show me.

Old Town Bar
Union Square · Est. 1892 · The Meagher Family
A spotlight on a piece of New York City history. Old Town Bar has held down Union Square since 1892. Today it's run by brothers Gerard and Matthew Meagher, who took over after their father Larry passed away — carrying forward the original mahogany, the dumbwaiter still running food up from the kitchen, the iconic wings, and the kind of century-old character you can't manufacture. Only inherit.
Union Square Est. 1892 Family Legacy

Featured
Little Maven
Chef Josh Capon's Latest · VCR Group
Chef Josh Capon's newest concept — the latest from the VCR Group portfolio that includes Fly Fish Club, Capon's Chophouse, Capon's Burgers, and Ito Tribeca. We started with the tuna tartare cannoli, then the 8oz prime burger topped with melted French raclette, truffle aioli, and freshly shaved black winter truffles — only ten made a day. Warm miso chocolate chip cookies and vanilla soft serve to close. In our opinion, Chef Josh Capon is the king of NYC comfort food hospitality.
VCR Group Chef Josh Capon Truffle Burger

Our Story

9
Years watching great concepts disappear

There was a bar and grill in NYC called 30 Love. One day it was gone. No announcement. No farewell. Nobody noticed until after. That feeling never left our founder.

MEAT Hospitality Group exists so no chef ever closes without being heard first. We give chefs and operators a real audience, real feedback, and real demand before they ever sign a lease.

"I never set out to fix the restaurant industry. I set out to make sure no chef ever closed without being heard first."

— Holden Krivelin, Founder & CEO · MEAT Hospitality Group

Built on a simple belief

Every chef, operator, and dreamer deserves a chance to test their idea before risking everything — to know if people want it before they bet it all.

Every dinner we host is proof of concept. Every guest is a voice. Every chef walks out of our feedback session with something no amount of planning could give them.

Good to Know

Frequently asked questions.

Is there a dress code?

No attitude, no dress code. Come as you are — just great food and the right people.

Can you accommodate dietary restrictions?

Yes — let us know when you reserve your seat and we'll work with the chef to accommodate allergies and dietary needs wherever possible.

How much does a Meatsperience cost?

Pricing varies by dinner and chef concept. Join the waitlist to be first to know when tickets for the next Meatsperience go live.

What's your cancellation policy?

Every seat at the table has value, so cancellations affect our chefs, venues, and partners. See the full guest expectations and cancellation details above for specifics.

Can I attend alone, or do I need to bring someone?

Come solo or bring a friend — both are welcome. Half the point of a Meatsperience is that strangers become a community by the end of the night.
