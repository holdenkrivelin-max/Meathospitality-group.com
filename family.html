SlowHours

All your redemptions in one place — your neighborhood's best deals, posted by the bars, restaurants, lounges, hotels and cafés themselves and rated by the neighbors who clipped them. No paywall, no queue — as long as you can afford it, you have access. Everybody is celebrated, and every clip is stamped. For venues, the result in one line: nearby customers in your slow hours — and you pay only when a clip redeems. Launching first in Kips Bay, founding venues first. Founded by Holden Krivelin and Adam — Adam wanted a coupon app for the whole world; Holden said do it neighborhood to neighborhood instead, because nobody else is doing it that way. Now with Ask Beck — tell Beck your neighborhood and he ranks the deals around you, with one line on why each is worth it; every search is counted, so venues see the demand for themselves. And the site itself now opens with The Night Walk — a night-sky scene where you walk to your favorite spot, tap the phone, and the SlowHours dashboard rises with the name and a big bright REDEEM button: press it, the venue confirms, and the confetti flies. Under it, Ask Beck still runs the step-by-step redemption demonstration — clip it, show it at your favorite spot, the venue confirms, and the REDEEMED stamp lands. New for owners: the site now runs a second demonstration, What You’re Missing — pick a slow window, watch the room sit empty while the street walks past, then post one deal and watch the tables fill.

Mission
We build the things we wish existed.

MHG Group is a software company. That sentence took forty-four apps to earn: a planner for your time, a map that lives through people, a home for your moments, tools for your money, your music, your work, your people. Every one of them exists because it was missing — from our lives first, and then, it turned out, from everyone's.

Our method is the mission: name every idea so none are lost, start every concept so none are forgotten, and bring each one to life — one at a time, in the open, only when it's ready for prime time. We don't chase the room behind the velvet rope. We build the door, and we leave it open.

"We're a company built for the future, by the future."

Accessibility is the new luxury. Software is the building — and yes, sometimes we open a room in it.
